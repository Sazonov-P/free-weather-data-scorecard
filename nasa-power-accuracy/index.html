How accurate is NASA POWER weather data?

Good for rain, behind ERA5 on temperature. At 807 weather stations in the US, Europe, Canada and Australia, 2017–2024, NASA POWER had the smallest error in weekly rain at 491 of 807 stations, more than ERA5 and ECMWF IFS together. Its typical weekly error ranged from 4.6 mm (Canada) to 5.5 mm (Europe). It was not the best for rain in California, Washington State, Denmark, Alberta and South Australia. For daily highs it won 114 of 804 stations, most of them in Australia, and for daily lows 73 of 804.

Exceptions

Where NASA POWER is not the best for rain

  • California: ECMWF IFS won 8 of 17 stations; NASA POWER 6. Typical weekly error: NASA POWER 4.0 mm, ECMWF IFS 3.8 mm.
  • Washington State: ECMWF IFS won 11 of 14 stations; NASA POWER 2. Typical weekly error: NASA POWER 3.9 mm, ECMWF IFS 3.4 mm.
  • Denmark: ERA5 won 12 of 18 stations; NASA POWER 1. Typical weekly error: NASA POWER 6.6 mm, ERA5 4.9 mm.
  • Alberta: ERA5 won 11 of 22 stations; NASA POWER 7. Typical weekly error: NASA POWER 4.5 mm, ERA5 3.9 mm.
  • South Australia: ERA5 won 20 of 32 stations; NASA POWER 0. Typical weekly error: NASA POWER 4.1 mm, ERA5 2.9 mm.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Stations: the national weather services of the US, seven European countries, Canada and Australia, each day counted the way the service counts it. Regions with at least 10 stations get their own page.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation, on the same days for all sources, 2017–2024. Typical error is the mean absolute error at the median station: of daily values for temperature, of weekly totals for rain.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them; NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Each edition explains its stations and day boundaries: US, Europe, Canada, Australia.
