How accurate is NASA POWER rainfall in South Australia?

Less accurate than ERA5 here. At 32 weather stations in South Australia, 2017–2024, NASA POWER was typically 4.1 mm off on weekly rain and 56% off on monthly totals; it did not have the smallest error at any of the 32 stations. ERA5 did best, at 20 of 32 stations, typically 2.9 mm off. For temperature, ECMWF IFS had the smallest error in the daily high at 12 of 32 stations.

South Australia up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.67 °C at Coober Pedy Airport to 4.02 °C at Nullarbor.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 1.32 °C here and 1.31 °C across the whole Australian edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 4.1 mm here and 5.2 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, October to March, weekly rain was off by 4.8 mm in NASA POWER, 3.0 mm in ERA5 and 3.5 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 35% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 30%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of the Bureau of Meteorology, with each day counted the way the service counts it (rain and daily lows over the 24 hours to 9 a.m. local time, daily highs over the 24 hours from 9 a.m.). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: Australian edition.
