Is ERA5 the best free temperature data in Western Australia?

A close race on daily highs. At 49 weather stations in Western Australia, 2017–2024, ERA5 and NASA POWER tied on the daily high, each with the smallest error at 17 of 49 stations; typical errors: ECMWF IFS 1.32 °C, ERA5-Land 1.37 °C, ERA5 1.38 °C, NASA POWER 1.41 °C. ERA5 had the smallest error in the daily low at 30 of 49 stations. For rain, NASA POWER had the smallest error in weekly rain at 22 of 49 stations.

Western Australia up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.74 °C at Dwellingup to 3.83 °C at Karratha Aero.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 1.38 °C here and 1.31 °C across the whole Australian edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 4.0 mm here and 5.2 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, October to March, weekly rain was off by 3.1 mm in NASA POWER, 3.5 mm in ERA5 and 3.8 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 30% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 29%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of the Bureau of Meteorology, with each day counted the way the service counts it (rain and daily lows over the 24 hours to 9 a.m. local time, daily highs over the 24 hours from 9 a.m.). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: Australian edition.
