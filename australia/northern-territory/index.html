Is ERA5 the best free temperature data in the Northern Territory?

A close race on daily highs. At 24 weather stations in the Northern Territory, 2017–2024, ECMWF IFS had the smallest error in the daily high at 10 of 24 stations; typical errors: ERA5 1.27 °C, ECMWF IFS 1.31 °C. ERA5 had the smallest error in the daily low at 13 of 24 stations. For rain, NASA POWER had the smallest error in weekly rain at 15 of 24 stations.

Northern Territory up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.75 °C at Tennant Creek Airport to 3.75 °C at Groote Eylandt Airport.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 1.27 °C here and 1.31 °C across the whole Australian edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 8.7 mm here and 5.2 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, October to March, weekly rain was off by 16.4 mm in NASA POWER, 17.8 mm in ERA5 and 18.6 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 41% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 37%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of the Bureau of Meteorology, with each day counted the way the service counts it (rain and daily lows over the 24 hours to 9 a.m. local time, daily highs over the 24 hours from 9 a.m.). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: Australian edition.
