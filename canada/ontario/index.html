ERA5 vs NASA POWER in Ontario

ERA5 for temperature, NASA POWER for rain. At 28 weather stations in Ontario, 2017–2024, ERA5 had the smallest error in the daily high at 15 of 28 stations, typically 1.07 °C off, and in the daily low at 19 of 28. NASA POWER had the smallest error in weekly rain at 15 of 28 stations, typically 6.9 mm off a week and 27% off on monthly totals.

Ontario up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.69 °C at Hamilton A to 2.77 °C at Gore Bay Climate.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 1.07 °C here and 1.12 °C across the whole Canada edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 6.9 mm here and 4.6 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, April to September, weekly rain was off by 8.4 mm in NASA POWER, 9.6 mm in ERA5 and 9.2 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 36% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 27%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of Environment and Climate Change Canada, with each day counted the way the service counts it (highs, lows and rain from 06:00 to 06:00 UTC). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: Canada edition.
