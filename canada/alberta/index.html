How accurate is NASA POWER rainfall in Alberta?

Less accurate than ERA5 here. At 22 weather stations in Alberta, 2017–2024, NASA POWER was typically 4.5 mm off on weekly rain and 39% off on monthly totals; it had the smallest error at 7 of 22 stations. ERA5 did best, at 11 of 22 stations, typically 3.9 mm off. For temperature, ERA5 had the smallest error in the daily high at 17 of 22 stations.

Alberta up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.93 °C at Edmonton Stony Plain CS to 6.76 °C at Jasper Warden, where the ERA5 grid point sits 697 m above the station.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 1.22 °C here and 1.12 °C across the whole Canada edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 4.5 mm here and 4.6 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, April to September, weekly rain was off by 6.5 mm in NASA POWER, 5.8 mm in ERA5 and 6.3 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 45% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 41%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of Environment and Climate Change Canada, with each day counted the way the service counts it (highs, lows and rain from 06:00 to 06:00 UTC). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: Canada edition.
