ERA5 vs NASA POWER in Minnesota

ERA5 for temperature, NASA POWER for rain. At 14 weather stations in Minnesota, 2017–2024, ERA5 had the smallest error in the daily high at 11 of 14 stations, typically 0.98 °C off, and in the daily low at 11 of 14. NASA POWER had the smallest error in weekly rain at all 14 stations, typically 5.4 mm off a week and 24% off on monthly totals.

Minnesota up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.83 °C at St Cloud RGNL AP to 2.54 °C at Grand Marais, where the ERA5 grid point sits 198 m above the station.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 0.98 °C here and 1.06 °C across the whole US edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 5.4 mm here and 5.5 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, April to September, weekly rain was off by 8.3 mm in NASA POWER, 11.0 mm in ERA5 and 10.0 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 34% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 33%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of NOAA (airport ASOS stations and the US Climate Reference Network), with each day counted the way the service counts it (midnight to midnight, local standard time). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: US edition.
