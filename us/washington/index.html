How accurate is NASA POWER rainfall in Washington State?

Less accurate than ECMWF IFS here. At 14 weather stations in Washington State, 2017–2024, NASA POWER was typically 3.9 mm off on weekly rain and 60% off on monthly totals; it had the smallest error at 2 of 14 stations. ECMWF IFS did best, at 11 of 14 stations, typically 3.4 mm off. For temperature, ERA5 had the smallest error in the daily high at 8 of 14 stations.

Washington State up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.73 °C at Spokane INTL AP to 3.72 °C at Omak, where the ERA5 grid point sits 460 m above the station.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 1.21 °C here and 1.06 °C across the whole US edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 3.9 mm here and 5.5 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, April to September, weekly rain was off by 2.7 mm in NASA POWER, 2.5 mm in ERA5 and 2.2 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 43% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 41%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of NOAA (airport ASOS stations and the US Climate Reference Network), with each day counted the way the service counts it (midnight to midnight, local standard time). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
