ERA5 vs NASA POWER in Austria

ERA5 for temperature, NASA POWER for rain. At 23 weather stations in Austria, 2017–2024, ERA5 had the smallest error in the daily high at 11 of 23 stations, typically 2.51 °C off, and in the daily low at 14 of 23. NASA POWER had the smallest error in weekly rain at 14 of 23 stations, typically 8.9 mm off a week and 28% off on monthly totals.

Austria up close

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.74 °C at Schöngrabern to 8.04 °C at Innsbruck Universität, where the ERA5 grid point sits 1048 m above the station.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 2.51 °C here and 1.17 °C across the whole European edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 8.9 mm here and 5.5 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, April to September, weekly rain was off by 11.4 mm in NASA POWER, 13.7 mm in ERA5 and 13.0 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 35% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 35%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of GeoSphere Austria, with each day counted the way the service counts it (highs and lows from 18:00 UTC the day before to 18:00 UTC, rain from 06:00 to 06:00 UTC). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: European edition.
