Accuracy check · 18 weather stations in Denmark · 2017–2024

How accurate is NASA POWER rainfall in Denmark?

Less accurate than ERA5 here. At 18 weather stations in Denmark, 2017–2024, NASA POWER was typically 6.6 mm off on weekly rain and 35% off on monthly totals; it had the smallest error at 1 of 18 stations. ERA5 did best, at 12 of 18 stations, typically 4.9 mm off. For temperature, ECMWF IFS had the smallest error in the daily high at 9 of 17 stations.

What stands out

  • ERA5's error in the daily high ranged from 0.59 °C at Årslev to 1.78 °C at Nexø Vest.
  • ERA5's typical error in the daily high was 0.85 °C here and 1.17 °C across the whole European edition; NASA POWER's weekly rain error was 6.6 mm here and 5.5 mm across the edition.
  • In the growing season, April to September, weekly rain was off by 6.1 mm in NASA POWER, 5.8 mm in ERA5 and 6.0 mm in ECMWF IFS.
  • Of the days NASA POWER showed rain (1 mm or more), 30% were dry at the station; for ERA5 it was 25%.

Method and limits

How we checked

  • Reference: the daily records of the Danish Meteorological Institute (DMI), with each day counted the way the service counts it (highs, lows and rain from midnight to midnight Danish time, summer time included). Every source is rebuilt into the same days.
  • Each source is read at the nearest grid point over land, without interpolation. All sources are compared on the same days, 2017–2024; ECMWF IFS is Open-Meteo's archive of the 9 km forecast model, which starts in 2017.
  • Anholt Havn counts for rain only: ERA5-Land has no land point near it, and temperatures are compared only where all four sources have data.
  • Many of these stations send their readings to the forecast centers, and ERA5 and the ECMWF forecasts use them. NASA POWER rain is corrected with gauge data. In a field between stations, errors may be larger.
  • Full method, maps and the farm-task tables for the whole edition: European edition.
